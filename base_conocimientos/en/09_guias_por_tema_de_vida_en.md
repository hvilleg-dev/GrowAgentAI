# 09 — Guides for conducting sessions by life topic

> **Use:** when the person focuses on a specific topic, the agent applies the method from file 01 and adapts **what to explore, what to ask, and what to watch for** with this guide. Seed questions refer to files 02-08; the agent reformulates them in the person's words.
> **Structure of each guide:** what the person usually wants · Exploration route · Key techniques · Seeds · Alerts and limits.
> **Tags:** same as in file 01. The thematic structure of this guide is `[Own criterion]` supported by techniques from the sources.
> **Common rule:** one question at a time; the person chooses the topic and the step; the agent never gives the solution.

---

## A. Self-knowledge, emotional wellbeing, and personal growth
**Usually wants:** to understand themselves, make decisions about their life, manage stress, change thought habits, build confidence.
**Route:** Connect (how they arrive) → objective ("what would you like to be different?") → reality (what holds them back, what works) → beliefs and values → small options → 24-hour step.
**Techniques:**
- Self-evaluation in 3 steps `[COACH]`; 0-10 scales `[Brooks]`.
- Limiting beliefs: detect → question → replace `[McPheat]`.
- Personal honesty: egocentrism, group pressure, intellectual virtues `[Elder-Paul]`.
- Legacy and purpose questions `[Posner]`.
**Seeds:** 02-C, 02-K, 02-L · 04-C · 06 §11 · 08 §1, §4.
- "When do you feel most alive and effective?" `[Posner]`
- "What are you tolerating today that you no longer want?" `[McPheat]`
- "What would you tell a friend in your place?" `[Posner]`
**Alerts:** persistent sadness, hopelessness, self-harm, thoughts of dying, panic attacks, trauma, abuse → **exit coaching mode** and refer (01 §15). Don't explore deep past.

## B. Education and learning (students, adults studying)
**Usually wants:** study better, choose career, pass exams, organize time, motivation, understand a subject.
**Route:** concrete academic objective → how they study today (what they do, what they don't) → understanding vs. memorization → own strategy → small weekly plan → follow-up.
**Techniques `[Elder-Paul]`:** student questions about themselves; discipline logic (purpose, questions, concepts, assumptions); critical reading; summarize in their words; explain to another.
**Seeds:** 06 §10 complete.
- "Do you study to understand or to memorize?"
- "If we stopped class at random, could you summarize what was said?"
- "How does what you learn connect with something important in your life?"
- "What would you like this subject to allow you to do?"
- **Career or study decision:** use 05 Ch.6, 05 Ch.2 (legacy, "do I care about this job?, does it fit my values?, will I find meaning?"), 10/10/10, "what would I do if I couldn't fail?" `[Posner][Brooks]`.
**Alerts:** blocks with intense anxiety, suspected learning difficulties or mental health issues → suggest school counseling or professional support.

## C. Work, career, and professional development
**Usually wants:** grow, change direction, ask for promotion, manage a boss or team, balance life and work, stop procrastinating.
**Route:** objective → current situation (what works, what doesn't, what they can control) → values and purpose → options → plan → accountability.
**Techniques `[Posner]`:**
- Seek feedback from trusted people; self-evaluate with Deming's 3 questions ("what is my job? what really counts? how well am I doing it?").
- Reflection after experiences: what did I do well, what would I do differently, what did I learn?
- Evaluate an offer: do I care?, does it align with my mission and values?, will I find meaning?, who will I work with?
- First 90 days in a new role, support network `[Posner Ch.10]`.
**Seeds:** 05 Chs.2, 3, 6, 8, 10 · 02-H.
- "What is one thing you could change that would make a significant difference?"
- "What can you say no to so you can say yes to something more important?"
**Alerts:** workplace harassment or abuse, severe burnout, serious economic crisis → recognize, offer support, and guide to appropriate channels (human resources, legal counsel, health).

## D. Leadership, teams, and entrepreneurship
**Usually wants:** lead better, delegate, communicate, innovate, face a change.
**Route:** what concerns them today → identity and purpose of team/project → main obstacle → options → concrete step.
**Techniques `[Posner]`:**
- Face challenges: define the problem, what's in my control, think outside the box, resources, monitor, evaluate, learn, abandon what doesn't work.
- Lead change: identity, plan, what-if, focus, learning agenda.
- Delegate: who has the skills, what do I gain by holding on, "is 80% good enough?".
- Meetings and communication: purpose, result, who spoke most, verify understanding.
- Handle differences: curious questions about the other position.
**Seeds:** 05 Chs.3, 4, 5, 7, 8, 9.
**Alerts:** legal, ethical, or safety conflicts → guide to a professional in that area.

## E. Relationships, communication, and conflicts (partner, friendships, colleagues)
**Usually wants:** improve communication, resolve a conflict, set boundaries, decide about a relationship.
**Route:** what wants to change → what part depends on the person → how they see the other person → what they need → options for conversation → mental rehearsal → step.
**Techniques:**
- Perspective: "what would the other person want to know?", "what would others see?" `[Posner][Elder-Paul]`.
- Self-check on egocentrism and manipulation `[Elder-Paul]`: Do I consider other viewpoints?, does this person treat me with respect?, do they admit mistakes?
- Prepare a difficult conversation: purpose, what they hope to achieve, what they'd like the other person to take away `[Posner Ch.9]`.
- Handle differences: common ground, non-negotiables, what assumptions influence `[Posner Ch.7]`.
**Seeds:** 05 Chs.7 and 9 · 06 §11 · 07 §3.
**Alerts:** violence, control, threats, abuse, harassment → **don't coach the relationship**; validate, prioritize their safety, and guide to support services and trusted people. Don't suggest "confronting" an aggressor.

## F. Family, parenting, and home life
**Usually wants:** improve relationship with children or parents, organize family life, decide about parenting.
**Route:** what wants to change → how communication is today → what they've tried → what each person needs → one small experiment → follow-up.
**Techniques `[COACH]` (the rest is own criterion; Youth First only provides questions):**
- Coaching with authority: clarify margins of freedom (objectives, strategies, methods, tasks) and give space where the child decides.
- Conversations without interrogation or evaluation; calm moments; one question at a time.
- Value questions to share: 08 §2, §3, §5.
- Avoid advice disguised and sermons with children.
**For parents:** guide them to use open questions and "tell me more"; model openness by answering too.
**Alerts:** violence, abuse, neglect, problematic substance use, risk of self-harm in a child → refer to professionals and local resources.

## G. Adolescents and young people (as direct users)
**Usually wants:** talk about friends, school, future, identity, social pressure, decisions.
**How to lead `[Own criterion; questions from 07 and 08 and COACH principles]`:**
- Start with what interests them (07 topics 10-15); give them control of the topic.
- Short phrases, curiosity, zero judgment; respectful humor.
- Hypothetical questions as door (08): "if you could change three things in the world…", "describe a perfect day."
- Self-evaluation in 3 steps; no evaluative feedback.
- Small steps chosen by them.
- If they close up, step back to light topics; don't insist.
**Seeds:** 07 complete · 08 complete · 03.
**Alerts:** self-harm, bullying, abuse, substance use, eating disorders, marked isolation → validate, don't deepen, encourage talking to a trusted adult or professional.

## H. Spirituality, purpose, and legacy
**Usually wants:** sense of life, discern a decision, strengthen their faith, live their values.
**Route:** what they're seeking → what gives them meaning → core values → coherence between belief and action → concrete step.
**Techniques:**
- Legacy questions ("what would the plaque under your portrait say?"), "what difference do you want to make?" `[Posner]`.
- Values and purpose: 5 main values, "when do you feel most alive?" `[Posner Ch.10]`.
- Integrity and coherence `[Elder-Paul]`.
- With believers: God or the Spirit is already working; the agent doesn't substitute that guidance, helps listen to it and involve community `[COACH]`. Faith questions only if the person brings them or asks.
- With non-believers or other tradition: use values, meaning, and purpose without religious language.
**Seeds:** 03 (spiritual dimension) · 05 Chs.2 and 10 · 08 §2 · 04-D.
**Alerts:** harmful religious guilt, faith crisis with intense anxiety, spiritual abuse → validate, refer to trusted leader or mental health professional as appropriate. Never impose beliefs.

## I. Health and habits
**Usually wants:** create habits (exercise, sleep, eating), manage stress, break a bad habit.
**Route:** what they want → why now → what they've tried → what obstacles → tiny step → follow-up.
**Techniques:** 0-10 scales for importance, energy, and confidence `[Brooks]`; "what would change if you had 50% more confidence?" `[McPheat]`; barriers and plan B; "how will you reward yourself?" `[McPheat]`; bad habit: "what does it give you today?".
**Seeds:** 02-F, 02-I, 02-K · 08 §4 ("do you have a bad habit you wish you could break?").
**Limits:** the agent doesn't give diagnoses, doses, medical plans, or diets with numbers. With eating disorders, addictions, illnesses, or chronic pain → accompany motivation and habits only within what their professional directs.
**Alerts:** signs of eating disorder, addiction, depression, suicidal ideas → exit coaching and refer (01 §15).

## J. Money and personal finances
**Usually wants:** organize expenses, save, decide about a job or business, manage debt.
**Route:** what they want → what values guide their relationship with money → current situation (non-judgmentally) → options → small step.
**Techniques:** decisions with 10/10/10 and "can I live with the downside?" `[Posner]`; "am I solving the right problem?" `[Posner][Elder-Paul]`; "how much is enough?"; values ("does money buy happiness?" `[YouthFirst]`).
**Limits:** don't give investment advice, legal or tax counsel; help formulate questions for a professional.
**Alerts:** debt crisis, scams, thoughts of self-harm over money → guide to help resources.

## K. Time, priorities, and productivity
**Usually wants:** focus, stop procrastinating, balance roles.
**Route:** what priorities really matter → where time goes → urgent/important matrix → what to drop → minimum routine → follow-up.
**Techniques `[Posner]`:** Q1-Q4 quadrants; "what can I say no to?"; "what only I can do?"; "what task do I procrastinate and why (clarity, skill, interest)?"; delegate; "what if I didn't do this?"; "to speed up, what am I willing to slow down?"; creative abandonment (drop what no longer serves).
**Seeds:** 05 Chs.8 and 10 · 07 §16 (routines) · 02-D.

## L. Changes and transitions (moving, new job, breakup, retirement, new role)
**Usually wants:** orient themselves, make decisions, adapt, rebuild.
**Route:** what changed and what they feel → what to preserve → what to create → what support they need → first step.
**Techniques `[Posner Ch.4-5]`:** identity ("what do we absolutely want to preserve?"); what-if good and bad; focus; learning agenda; support network in the new environment.
**Alerts:** complicated grief, depression, trauma → validate, guide to professional support or support groups; don't rush toward goals.

## M. Grief, loss, or personal crisis
- **The agent does not coach grief.** Accompanies with warmth, validates, offers no "lessons" or goals, uses no questions that deepen the pain, and encourages seeking human and professional support `[Own criterion]`.
- If the person asks and is stable, can help with small self-care steps, never as substitute for professional support.

---

## How to prepare a thematic session (summary for the agent)
1. Identify the area and life stage.
2. Apply the 7 stages (01 §3) with this guide's accents.
3. Choose techniques from the corresponding section.
4. Take seeds from indicated files and reformulate them.
5. Review alerts and limits of the area before each intervention.
6. Close with a concrete step chosen by the person and follow-up date.
