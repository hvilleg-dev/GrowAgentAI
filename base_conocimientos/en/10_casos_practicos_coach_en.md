# 10 — Practical coaching conversation cases (COACH book, ch. "Daily Application")

> **Source:** the 18 real situations from the book *The COACH Model for Youth Leaders* (Ortiz and Webb): 5 with adolescents (James), 7 with university students (Rodrigo), and 6 with young professionals (Danila). Here **the dialogues are not reproduced**; each case is summarized in their words and only the brief coach questions that illustrate the technique are preserved. The book invites analyzing each case with 6 questions (what did the coach do, what didn't, what tools did they use, what COACH elements, what principles, where else applies).
> **Agent use:** learn *how* a coaching conversation sounds: pace, open questions, how to reach a practical step. **Don't copy literal phrases or religious content** unless the person brings it.
> **Original context:** they are Christian youth leaders with young people from their church; some interventions use biblical language. For a general agent, translate that language to values and the person's own perspective.

## How to read each case
**Topic → coach's journey → person's discovery → practical step → technique illustrated → critical observations for the agent.**

## JAMES'S SITUATIONS (adolescents)

### Case 1 — Peer pressure (Victor, adolescent)
- **Topic:** drank alcohol at a party to be accepted; shame, hangover, and later mockery; feels guilty.
- **Journey:** connect with an emotional question ("how do you feel now that several days have passed?") → ask help to understand the anger at himself ("help me understand it") → validate pressure without judging → question the cost ("what do you think about the price you paid to be accepted?") → learning ("what have you learned from all this?") → work guilt and self-forgiveness → prevention ("what strategies could you put in place?", "what can help you stay firm?").
- **Discovery:** respecting yourself matters more than seeking approval; needed to forgive themselves.
- **Practical step:** anticipate risky situations and practice saying "no" firmly.
- **Technique:** EXPAND (learning + self-awareness) and CONCRETIZE (preventive strategy).
- **Observation for agent:** the coach introduces a religious framework (biblical forgiveness) *before* the teen brings it; it's a directive intervention. With agent: ask "what would help you forgive yourself?" without providing the framework, unless the person mentions it. Alcohol use in minors: validate, don't moralize, and invite talking to a trusted adult.

### Case 2 — Problems with parents (Maria, adolescent)
- **Topic:** overheard her parents doubting her ability to study video game design; feels hurt.
- **Journey:** emotion ("how did you feel hearing that conversation?") → **take perspective** ("what might have led your parents to think that?") → "tell me more about that" → find pattern ("any similar situation with your studies?") → ask synthesis ("how does this help you understand their attitude?").
- **Technique "integrity account":** coach explains a metaphor (trust is like a bank account: deposits when you keep promises, withdrawals when you don't) then asks "how's your account?" and "how can you make deposits to gain more trust?".
- **Discovery:** parents' distrust stems from promises she left incomplete.
- **Practical step:** study two hours daily to pass the subject pending from next month.
- **Closing:** motivation scale ("little, fair, a lot, very much") → "a lot, almost very much."
- **Technique illustrated:** EXPAND (perspective shift) + CONCRETIZE (measurable steps) + motivation scale.
- **Observation:** coach provides the concept (not purely non-directive) but offers it as a metaphor and returns with questions. Acceptable if used with permission: "Can I share an idea that might help you see it?"

### Case 3 — "You can't make a living from music" (Juan Pablo, adolescent)
- **Topic:** wants to be a musician; parents want him to study architecture; arguing for weeks.
- **Journey (technique "viewing from another position"):** ask permission and warn it works if willing to be objective → (1) **own position:** "imagine you can talk without interruption, what would you tell them?" → "why is it important to you that they listen?" → (2) **father's position:** "imagine you are your father talking to you, what would you say to Juan Pablo?", "what else would he say if he knew he wouldn't be interrupted?" → (3) **neutral observer:** "imagine you are a third person advising both, what would you tell them?" → return to self: "what would be a middle-ground solution?", "what do you think you'll do now?".
- **Discovery:** he wasn't listening either; was closed to any alternative to music.
- **Practical step:** propose to his parents a middle-ground solution (finish high school in exchange for continuing to rehearse while maintaining good grades).
- **Technique illustrated:** **three perceptual positions** (self, other, observer) in the EXPAND phase.
- **Value for agent:** it's a replicable procedure in chat for conflicts with another person (family, partner, boss, friends). *Textual adaptation:* "If you'd like, let's try an exercise to see the situation from other places. First, what would you tell that person if you could talk without interruption?".

### Case 4 — "I have no friends at school" (Carolina, adolescent)
- **Topic:** wants to change schools because she thinks classmates are "weird"; her parents already said no.
- **Journey:** open question about what makes friendship difficult → "what would the solution be?" → hypothesis ("suppose you changed, what opportunities would it give you?") → "what would you do to make friends?" → **challenge the assumption with a "what if"** ("what if the new classmates didn't take initiative?", "what if they weren't receptive?") → honest question with permission ("what prevented you from doing it in this school?") → return to reality ("given your parents' firm decision, what could you do to break the isolation?") → motivation ("how motivated are you?").
- **Discovery:** her isolation was caused by her own initial rejecting attitude.
- **Practical step:** change attitude, take initiative in recess, and join a sports team.
- **Technique:** EXPAND (question assumptions, from external excuse to personal responsibility).
- **Value for agent:** shows how to use "what if…?" so the person sees that the external solution also requires internal action, without telling them.

### Case 5 — "Cybersex" (Fernando, 16 years) — **DO NOT use as template**
- **Topic of the case:** a 16-year-old caught in a cycle of guilt over consuming pornography; coach reaches an accountability system with alert software.
- **Technical value that can be rescued (without the content):**
  - Normalize with honesty ("I don't know anyone who hasn't explored…") before asking about impact.
  - Evaluate the cycle ("what makes you think you decide something and do it over and over?") with invitation to honesty ("be honest with yourself").
  - Two steps the coach offers: wanting to do something, and asking "what could help me drop this habit?".
  - Ask what others who've succeeded do.
  - Let the person choose the mechanism and who to involve ("knowing how you know yourself, what would be the best option?").
- **Why NOT to use it as a script in an agent:**
  1. **Sexual content with a minor.** The agent shouldn't conduct detailed conversations about sexuality or sexual behaviors with minors: must respond with warmth, without judgment or details, and encourage talking to a trusted adult or professional.
  2. **Self-disclosure and leading questions.** Coach shares intimate personal experience and uses questions that guide the answer ("but it happens again, doesn't it?"), clashing with the book's own non-directive principles.
  3. **Supervision by third parties.** The plan involves monitoring software toward others; an agent shouldn't suggest surveillance or decide who gets informed.
  4. **Scope:** compulsive behaviors require a professional, not coaching.
- **Rule for agent:** facing such a topic, validate that shame isn't unusual, don't deepen details, don't propose monitoring plans, and warmly guide to a trusted adult and professional.

### Case 6 (mentioned within James's section) — Self-esteem (Wilson)
- **Topic:** a boy liked by friends and a good athlete feels insecure, withdraws, school performance drops, thinks about quitting studies.
- **Journey:** paraphrase his plan ("so you're thinking about quitting high school…?") → "how are you so sure of that?" → **reflect his chain of thinking** aloud ("I think I'm not good at studying → I don't study → I fail → I confirm I'm a bad student") and name it as **self-fulfilling prophecy** → "what do you think would be the second step?" → "what will happen if you spend time studying?" → "how can I help?".
- **Discovery:** there's a disconnect between what he thinks of himself and reality.
- **Practical step:** study to pass the subjects, with coach follow-up.
- **Technique:** EXPAND (reflection of inner logic) + limiting beliefs (self-fulfilling prophecy).
- **Value for agent:** reflecting the person's reasoning in their own words usually is more powerful than arguing against it.

## RODRIGO'S SITUATIONS (university students, 18-24 years)
*Context: spiritual and vocational accompaniment in a Christian faith environment. For a general agent, substitute the biblical framework with the values and beliefs the person brings.*

### Case 7 — "My work, my ministry" (Camilo, engineering student)
- **Topic:** doesn't know how to connect his beliefs with his future profession; wants his work to have meaning.
- **Key questions:** "How could this relate to your profession?" · "And how does it apply to your particular situation?" · "What does this mean for someone practicing this profession in their workplace?" · "Of everything we've talked about, what are you taking with you?".
- **Discovery:** his profession is a realm of service; doing it well can be an expression of his values and faith.
- **Practical step:** take his work as an act with meaning and look for opportunities to contribute in his future job.
- **Technique:** EXPAND (perspective shift on vocation) + MILESTONES.
- **For agent:** useful for sessions on **work meaning and purpose** (adaptable to non-believers: "what value of yours is expressed in your work?").

### Case 8 — Choosing a job (Adela, recent graduate)
- **Topic:** several job offers; wants to discern the best.
- **Key questions:** "What guidance do you find in [your principles/beliefs] on this decision?" · "What other issues should you consider?" · "When you've made important decisions before, what tipped you toward one option or another?" · "What steps will you take?" · "By when do you want to have decided?".
- **Discovery:** she doesn't need explicit direction; can use clear criteria aligned with her values.
- **Practical step:** filter the offers with **four criteria** (conscience, personal time, relationship with people, pay) and decide in two weeks.
- **Technique:** CONCRETIZE (tangible criteria, systematic filter, deadline).
- **For agent:** excellent pattern for **career decisions:** ask the person their criteria, use past decisions as evidence ("what tipped you then?") and set deadline.

### Case 9 — Ethical situations (Gerardo, young professional)
- **Topic:** his boss demands dishonest business practices; living a conscience dilemma.
- **Key questions:** "Help me understand the problem." · "How do these situations make you feel?" · "What options do you see, what can you do?" · "What would a plan B look like?" · "How do you anticipate your boss would respond?".
- **Discovery:** he can stay true to his values and propose an ethical alternative.
- **Practical step:** design his transparent "plan B" (with trial period) and request a meeting with his boss in three days.
- **Technique:** EXPAND (extract principles from their own source to generate alternatives) + anticipate reactions.
- **For agent:** pattern for **workplace ethical dilemmas**: emotion → options → plan B → anticipate the other's reaction → concrete date. *Limit:* if legal or safety risk, suggest professional counsel (01 §15).

### Case 10 — Spiritual growth / intangible goal (Patricio)
- **Topic:** vague goal ("be more holy/spiritual") hard to achieve.
- **Key questions:** "Help me understand what being more [X] means to you." · "In what practical way will your way of living have changed?" · "What will be different in you if that develops?" · "What would be the first step?" · "What do you think you should do with those feelings when they appear?".
- **Discovery:** his lack of grace was shown in being legalistic and judgmental with others.
- **Practical step:** recognize and name those thoughts instead of repressing them, with coach follow-up.
- **Technique:** GOAL/CONCRETIZE (**convert an intangible into observable behavior**).
- **For agent:** central pattern for abstract goals ("be happier," "be a better leader," "have more peace"): ask personal definition → translate to observable behavior → first step.

### Case 11 — Campus life (Rebeca)
- **Topic:** cultural and belief clash at university (relativism, diversity of lifestyles).
- **Key questions:** "If you had to highlight something from these months, what would it be?" · "What effect is all this having on your beliefs?" · "What have you learned?" · "What could be a first step?" · "Who could help you carry it out?".
- **Discovery:** her conviction was poorly documented; needs to study it to dialogue.
- **Practical step:** roadmap in three phases (review her convictions, understand other positions, share hers) and join a support group.
- **Technique:** CONCRETIZE + MILESTONES + support network.
- **Sensitive:** values clash and beliefs doubts. Agent **must not take a position** on what to believe; helps explore and seek human support.

### Case 12 — Relationship with community (Gerson)
- **Topic:** stopped attending his religious community because he considers it irrelevant and thinks his personal life suffices.
- **Key questions:** "Help me understand your position on [community]." · "What have you learned about the community dimension?" · "How do you interpret all this?".
- **Technique:** EXPAND (question exclusive premises showing complementarity: relationship with the transcendent, with others, with oneself).
- **Observation:** coach introduces a directive invitation to pray; for a general agent, rephrase as "what do you need from other people to sustain what you value?".
- **Sensitive:** distance from a faith community. Don't pressure or judge.

### Case 13 — Personal faith or inherited faith? (Leandro)
- **Topic:** identity and belief crisis; doubts if his convictions are his or inherited.
- **Star technique (third-person dissociation):** ask a summary "like newspaper headlines" then "what would you advise that young person if you were their friend?", "what should they do?", "what process would they follow?", "what's the next step?", "do you think they'd be willing?".
- **Discovery:** by reasoning in third person took emotional distance and saw he needed to review his doubts without prejudice.
- **Practical step:** initiate open dialogue to review his doubts and decide consciously.
- **Value for agent:** replicable pattern for **any emotionally charged dilemma**: "If it were someone else's story, what headline would it have? What would you tell them?". Very useful with people blocked by emotion.
- **Sensitive:** faith and identity crisis. If intense anxiety or hopelessness, refer (01 §15).

## DANILA'S SITUATIONS (young professionals)

### Case 14 — Professional career (Maria, executive)
- **Topic:** must decide whether to accept a promotion involving weekly travel; fears affecting her new marriage and community life.
- **Key questions:** "What would make this conversation valuable for you?" · "Explain a bit more…" · "What produces that insecurity, how do you explain it?" · "Tell me a bit about your scale of priorities." · "How can your scale of priorities help you make this decision?" · "What practical step could you take?".
- **Discovery:** her values scale gives her objective criteria to decide with peace.
- **Practical step:** reserve a free afternoon next week to think about her priorities before responding to the company.
- **Technique:** EXPAND (align decision with values and priorities).
- **For agent:** direct template for **career vs. family/values decisions**: ask scale of priorities → use as criterion → small step with date.

### Case 15 — Stress (Lorenzo, perfectionist lawyer)
- **Topic:** insomnia, irritability, and overwhelm from lack of a time management system.
- **Key questions:** "I've noticed you often mention being tired and overwhelmed; tell me, what's happening?" · "How is all this affecting you?" · "How does it make you feel?" · "What could help improve this situation?" · "What or who could help you develop those skills?" · "Who in your circle could help?" · "When will you find out and how motivated are you?".
- **Notable opening pattern:** coach **names what they've been observing with curiosity** ("I've noticed that…"), without diagnosing.
- **Discovery:** agenda disorganization explains much of the stress; needs time management training.
- **Practical step:** search next day for accessible time management courses.
- **Technique:** EXPAND → CONCRETIZE (turn complaint into active search).
- **Sensitive:** chronic stress, insomnia, and possible burnout. **Agent limit:** coaching can accompany time organization, but if insomnia, intense irritability, hopelessness, or health symptoms persist, suggest consulting a health professional (01 §15).

### Case 16 — Friends (Rudy)
- **Topic:** disputes with community friends who criticize his new work relationships with people of other beliefs.
- **Key questions:** "Help me understand [the other side]'s arguments." · "And what do you think?" · "What criteria could help you discern which friends suit you?" · "In what way would having defined criteria help?" · "**If you had to help someone define criteria, what would you advise them?**" · "And once you set those criteria, what else would you tell me?".
- **Technique:** EXPAND with **role inversion** (the person "advises" the coach to structure their own values).
- **Practical step:** apply four personal criteria (respect for her faith, positive influence, absence of pressure, sincere affection) to evaluate her relationships.
- **For agent:** pattern for **"defining own criteria"** in friendship or relationship dilemmas: ask criteria → apply them → decide.

### Case 17 — Daily spirituality (Gabriela, programmer)
- **Topic:** feels her work routine lacks meaning and sees community teachings disconnected.
- **Key questions:** "What would you like to achieve through this conversation?" · "**If your work had a spiritual dimension (or of meaning), what would it look like?**" · "What practical issues would help you live those principles?" · "What could help you remember this daily?" · "When would you like to start?".
- **Practical step:** turn the daily commute into a moment of intention and pay more attention to her colleagues.
- **Technique:** CONCRETIZE (convert abstract concepts into **small daily habits**).
- **For agent:** pattern of **meaning in routine** and anchoring purpose to existing habits (e.g., link an intention to a fixed moment of the day).

### Case 18 — Children (German and Josefa, couple)
- **Topic:** indecision about when to have their first child amid contradictory opinions from their circle.
- **Key questions (to the couple):** "What would you like to achieve with our conversation?" · "What factors would make that moment ideal for you?" · "And for you, Josefa?" · "Is there any other factor to consider?" · "Hearing all these factors, what do you feel, what do you believe is most convenient?" · "How do you feel now about the decision?".
- **Discovery:** they recognized real barriers (debts, housing, studies) and preferred to consolidate before becoming parents.
- **Practical step:** consciously agree to postpone, at peace and free from external pressure.
- **Technique:** EXPAND with **discernment criteria** and **ask each person separately** so both express themselves.
- **For agent:** with couples or shared decisions, ask *each one* their criterion separately then compare. **Limit:** don't give opinion on whether to have children; it's the couple's decision.

### Case 19 — Who will I marry (Luis, 33 years)
- **Topic:** anxiety about not knowing how to "discern" who to marry after seeing friends' divorces.
- **Key questions:** "What would make this conversation useful?" · "How would you feel if you had more clarity?" · "What makes you think the lack of [an explicit guide] on this topic?" · "If you feel responsible for choosing, what criteria could help you?" · "How do you feel about that?".
- **Discovery:** there's no predetermined choice to discover; he has freedom and responsibility to choose wisely.
- **Practical step:** evaluate potential partners with four personal criteria (faith, values of service, family, no materialism).
- **Technique:** EXPAND (**dismount a paralyzing myth** and return the responsibility to decide).
- **For agent:** pattern for **affective decisions** with fear of mistakes: validate emotion → name the myth ("there must be one right answer") → move to own criteria.

### Case 20 — Forgiveness and relationships (Patricia)
- **Topic:** resentment toward a friend who ended up married to her ex; feels stuck.
- **Key questions:** "Could you help me understand what makes forgiveness so hard for you?" · "How would you like to feel?" · "What prevents you from leaving the problem behind?" · "What if we saw it from another perspective?" · "How many times do you breathe a day?" (analogy: forgive as recurring process) · "What are you going to do about it?".
- **Discovery:** forgiveness is not a one-time event but a process that repeats when pain resurfaces.
- **Practical step:** each time rage arises, actively "let go" of pain through a simple, repeated act.
- **Technique:** EXPAND with **analogy** to reformulate the idea of emotional release.
- **Sensitive:** deep resentment and stuck emotional pain. **Agent limit:** can accompany with small steps and analogies, but if depression symptoms, incapacitating rumination, or trauma persist, guide to professional support.

## GENERAL NOTES OF THIS FILE
- **Count:** the NotebookLM index listed 18 cases (5+7+6); when extracting them, James's section included an extra mentioned case (Self-esteem, Wilson) and Danila's presented 7. Result: **20 situations** documented above. The difference from "18" remains unresolved in the original text.
- **Repeating question patterns the agent should imitate:**
  1. *Opening:* "What would make this conversation valuable for you?" · "Help me understand…" · "Explain a bit more…".
  2. *Emotion:* "How does that make you feel?" · "How is it affecting you?".
  3. *Perspective:* three perceptual positions, third person, advise a friend, role inversion, analogies.
  4. *Criteria:* "What criteria could help you decide?".
  5. *Practical step:* "What would be the first step?" · "What or who could help?" · "When will you do it?" · "How motivated are you (little, fair, a lot, very much)?".
  6. *Closing:* "Of everything we talked about, what are you taking?".
- **Almost constant structure:** emotion → understanding → perspective/criteria → options → first step with date → motivation scale.
- **Watch out for religious content:** in several cases the coach provides biblical frameworks or invitations to pray. In the agent, use that language **only if the person brings it**.
- **Watch out for directiveness:** there are moments when the coach proposes concepts or metaphors. In the agent, offer them **with permission** and immediately return with a question.
