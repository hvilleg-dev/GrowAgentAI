# 07 — Questions to connect with adolescents and young people (icebreaker → coaching)

> Source: "300 conversation questions for adolescents" (conversation resource originally created for adolescent classes). **These are not coaching questions by themselves:** they serve to **connect, build trust, and get people talking** (phase C of C.O.A.C.H.). Each topic includes a **bridge** at the end toward a deeper coaching question.
> Criteria for pruning: useful questions for getting to know the person were preserved; merely trivia or language-teaching questions were omitted (full "English" section, zodiac sign/Chinese year/element, phone brand, questions about computer viruses or MP4s). Questions repeated across topics appear only once.

## What the source does and doesn't say (verified against the text)
- **Origin and audience:** downloadable resource from a language teaching book; Chapter 15 "Getting Them to Talk." It's aimed at **teachers and tutors** working with adolescents (tested at B1 to C1 language levels).
- **Declared purpose:** listen to each student the first week, practice fluency and prepare topics throughout the year, and serve as material for small groups and tutoring.
- **Usage instructions it does give:** use blocks of 15 questions per topic for a long oral activity, or blocks of 5 to deepen and re-ask; **if a question doesn't fit or doesn't match the group, context, or culture, skip it and move to the next.**
- **What it does NOT say:** nothing about listening, tone, trust, or coaching. The following rules are **own criterion** (`[Own criterion]`) to adapt the resource to a coaching agent.

## Rules of use for the agent with adolescents (13-19 years) `[Own criterion]`
1. **Start light** (topic the young person chooses or mentions) and go deep only when there is trust.
2. **Don't interrogate:** alternate a question with a brief and authentic comment. If a question doesn't fit, skip it and move to another.
3. **Give them control:** "What would you like to talk about today: your week, your friends, school, your plans?"
4. **Don't judge** tastes, music, social networks, or friendships. Ask with real curiosity.
5. **Respect privacy:** don't insist on family or intimate things if the young person closes up.
6. **Alert signals** (persistent sadness, isolation, self-harm, bullying, violence, abuse, substance use): stop the icebreaker, respond with warmth, validate what they feel, and encourage talking to a trusted adult or professional. Don't continue with goals.
7. Use simple language, short sentences, no lectures.

## 1. About me (identity and self-concept)
- How would you describe yourself? · Think of three adjectives that describe you.
- What things are you really into?
- Is there anything in this world you can't stand?
- Are you a relaxed person? Patient?
- How many people really know you?
- Is your life the way you want it to be?
- What do your teachers say about you?
- Why do your friends enjoy being with you?
- Do you laugh at other people often?
- If you were an animal, what would you be? · What is your favorite color? Why?
- *Bridge → coaching:* "Of what you just told me, what part of you would you like others to know better?" · "If your life could change in one thing only, what would it be?"

## 2. Family
- Who do you get along with best in your family? · Do you clash with anyone?
- Who do you spend the most time with? · What is the best thing about your family?
- In character and appearance, who do you resemble?
- Do you always eat together? Watch TV together? Go places together?
- When do you spend time with aunts, uncles, and grandparents? Do you have a favorite aunt or uncle? Do you have cousins your age? Describe one.
- Does your family have a pet?
- What do you mainly talk about with your parents?
- How much time do you spend alone each day? Do you like it?
- *Bridge:* "What would you like to be different in how you talk at home?" · "What do you need from your family that you're not getting today?"

## 3. Friends
- How do your friends describe you? · Why are your friends important?
- What do you do when you get together with your friends? · When do you see your best friends?
- Do your friends go to your school or others?
- Describe one of your best friends. · Who is your newest friend?
- What qualities do you look for in a friend?
- When you go out, how many people are usually in your group?
- How do you communicate electronically with your friends?
- How many online friends do you have? Have you made a friend on the internet?
- Do you sleep over at friends' houses?
- *Bridge:* "With whom do you feel like yourself for real?" · "What kind of friend do you want to be to others?" · "Is there someone in your group you have trouble being with?"

## 4. Neighborhood and environment
- What is the best thing about your neighborhood? What is the worst? · Do you really like your neighborhood?
- How well do you get along with your neighbors?
- What are the most well-known places in your neighborhood?
- When you go out with friends, do you stay in your neighborhood? Are your friends from your neighborhood?
- Have you lived in your neighborhood your whole life? Has it changed in any way?
- If you had to live in another neighborhood, which would it be?
- *Bridge:* "How does where you live influence how you feel and what you dream?"

## 5. My room (personal space)
- How much time do you spend in your room? · Do you share a room or have one to yourself?
- How private is it? · Do you keep your room tidy?
- Do you like your room? How would you improve it?
- What do you have on your walls? What do you see from your window?
- *Bridge:* "What place gives you peace when everything gets complicated?"

## 6. School (educational stage)
- What year are you in? How long have you been at your current school?
- What is the best thing about your school? What is the worst?
- Do you find school relaxing or stressful?
- What is your favorite place at school and why?
- Who do you sit with in class? Where?
- Who was your favorite teacher in elementary school?
- Do you usually arrive early, on time, or late?
- What are the first five things you do when you arrive at school?
- If you could change one thing about your school, what would it be?
- *Bridge:* "What subject or activity makes you feel most capable?" · "What would you like to learn that no one teaches you today?" · See file 06 §10 (learning and studying).

## 7. Future (decision-making stage)
- What is your biggest ambition? · Where do you see yourself living in 15 years?
- Would you like to go to university? · Do you have an idea what job you'd like to do?
- Are you tired of being asked what you'll be when you grow up?
- What is your priority for this year? · What are your plans for the next 5 years?
- Do you prefer working for a company or for yourself?
- Do you think you'll want kids?
- Do you prefer sharing an apartment with friends or living alone?
- What cities would you be happy living in?
- What would you like to learn more about in the future?
- Do you make "to do" lists? · When the year starts, do you make resolutions?
- What matters more: the past, the present, or the future?
- *Bridge:* "If there were no fear or pressure, what would you choose?" · "What small step could you take this week toward that?" · Connect with 02-H (action) and 03 (Concrete).

## 8. Early childhood (memories and roots)
- Where did you grow up? · Were you a calm or difficult baby?
- Did you go to daycare or preschool?
- What was your favorite toy? Did you have a stuffed animal?
- What was your favorite show as a kid? · Do you have a favorite photo from when you were little?
- Do you remember your first day of elementary school? What was it like? Do you remember your first teacher?
- Who used to play a lot with you when you were little?
- What was your favorite food?
- *Bridge:* "What did you like about yourself as a kid that you sometimes forget today?"

## 9. Food and health
- Is there a food you especially like or can't stand? · Are you allergic to any?
- What is your all-time favorite dish?
- Do you prefer vegetables or fruit? Do you like sweets?
- What do you look for when eating at a restaurant?
- Do you eat standing up or in your bedroom?
- What is a perfect breakfast for you?
- *Note:* if signs appear of restriction, binging, guilt about eating, or body comparison, don't give numbers or diet plans; listen with warmth and guide toward a trusted adult and professional (NAED if in the US; if not, local services).

## 10. Music
- Do you have a favorite band or singer? What are your three favorite styles?
- When do you usually listen to music? Where do you get it?
- Do you play an instrument?
- When was the last time you heard live music?
- Does the music you listen to influence the clothes you wear?
- Do all your friends listen to the same music as you? · Do you listen to music with your parents?
- Can you dance to your favorite music?
- *Bridge:* "What song represents you today? What does it say to you?"

## 11. Sports and physical activity
- Do you play sports at school or outside? What sport do you enjoy most?
- Do you think sports should be required at school?
- Is there a sport you hate doing? Any sport you like to watch but not play?
- Who is your favorite sports celebrity?
- Do you look forward to physical education class?
- Do you like strategy games like chess?
- Have you done any martial arts?
- *Bridge:* "What does sports (or would) give you that you don't find anywhere else?"

## 12. Internet and social media
- What three things do you use the internet for most? · What bothers you most about the internet?
- What was the last thing you downloaded?
- On average, how much time do you spend online a day?
- How many social media accounts do you have?
- Do you have your own computer or tablet? · Do you have internet at school? How is the connection at home?
- Do your parents try to restrict your internet access?
- Who of your friends knows the most about the internet?
- Do you know anyone with a blog or their own website?
- *Bridge:* "How do you feel after spending a long time on social media?" · "What do you want your social media to say about you?"

## 13. Phones
- Do you have a phone? When did you get your first mobile?
- How much time do you spend calling or using it each day? Do you use it for anything else?
- Who pays the bill? Is there a usage limit?
- Where is your phone right now?
- What was the last message you sent?
- Can you think of five reasons why you call people?
- Do you think kids under ten need a mobile?
- *Bridge:* "What would happen if you went a whole day without your phone?"

## 14. Programs, series, and movies
- Are you watching any series right now? · What is the best show ever for you?
- Do you watch TV while eating?
- What is the most popular series among your friends?
- What was the last movie you saw at the cinema? What type of movie do you like most?
- What channel do you watch most? · Who is your favorite actress or actor?
- What makes a movie good for you?
- *Bridge:* "Which character do you identify with? What do you admire about them?"

## 15. Hobbies and free time
- What is your number one hobby? What else interests you?
- How much time do you dedicate to your favorite hobby?
- Is it exhausting or relaxing? Mental or physical?
- Do your friends share the same hobby as you?
- Is there a hobby you'd like to take up?
- Do you cook, build, create things?
- Is studying a hobby? Shopping? Graffiti?
- *Bridge:* "When do you lose track of time because you're enjoying what you do?" (hint of talents and purpose)

## 16. Weekends and routines
**Weekends**
- What time do you wake up? · Which night do you prefer going out, Friday or Saturday?
- If you have homework, when do you do it?
- What did you do and what will you do this weekend?
- What is the best and worst thing about weekends?
- Do you eat something special? · Do you visit your family?
- Do you get bored sometimes? · What would your perfect weekend be?
- Are Sundays the same as Saturdays for you?

**Routines**
- How long does it take you to get ready in the morning? What are the first five things you do?
- What motivates you to wake up? Who or what wakes you?
- How many hours do you sleep? Do you go to bed at the same time?
- What time do you eat?
- Describe your daily routine in a minute. Is your weekend routine different?
- How do you remember important things you need to do?
- Do you think you have enough free time?
- How would you like to change your routine? · Is it relaxing or boring? · What do you like about it?
- *Bridge:* "If you could design a perfect weekday, what would you change first?" · Alert: if persistent insomnia, major habit changes, or apathy appear, explore carefully and suggest support.

## 17. Traveling and vacations
- Did you go somewhere for vacation last summer? · Where do you want to visit in the future?
- What is the longest trip you've made?
- Do you like camping? · What is best about staying in hotels or hostels?
- Do you take school trips? · Do you take lots of photos? Do you write postcards?
- Do you take bus or train every day?
- Have you made a friend on vacation? · Do you read while traveling?
- Is it easy for you to sleep in other places?
- Would you go on vacation by yourself?
- When was the last time you missed a bus, train, or flight?
- *Bridge:* "What are you looking for when you travel that's missing from your daily life?"

## 18. Animals and pets
- Do you have a favorite pet? A favorite wild animal?
- Will you get a pet when you leave home?
- Do you like documentaries about wildlife? · What animals are most beautiful to you?
- Have you cleaned a fishbowl or cage? · Have you ridden a horse or camel?
- *Bridge:* "What do you care for, or would like to care for? What does that teach you about yourself?"
