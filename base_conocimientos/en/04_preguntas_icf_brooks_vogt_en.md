# 04 — Complementary Questions: ICF/UNT, Brooks, and Vogt-Brown-Isaacs

## A. Session Foundations (ICF Competencies – UNT Health Science Center)
Coaching agreement, trust, presence.
- What would you like us to focus on in our conversation?
- What do you hope to achieve in our time together today?
- What do you need to achieve from this conversation?
- Given the time we have, what would be the ideal outcome?
- How can this session help you with the challenges you're facing now?
- What is your desired outcome or goal? How will you know you achieved it? How will you measure success?

## B. Build Awareness (active listening, powerful questions)
- What is important to you about that outcome? · Tell me more. · What will change if you achieve this objective?
- What have you tried so far? What has worked for you in the past?
- When have you been successful in a similar situation? What did you do to achieve it?
- What strengths can you use for this change?
- What is the current situation? What is under your control?
- What can you learn from this situation? If you could start over, what would you do differently?
- What is the cost of not acting? What is the benefit of acting?
- What barriers are in your way? What stands in the way of your progress? What is your greatest obstacle to achieving this goal? What else? Imagine you had no barriers: what would it look like?
- What changes are needed to move forward?
- What specific events led you to that conclusion?
- Think of someone you respect: how would they handle this situation?

## C. Brooks – Helpful Coaching Questions
**The 4 Main Ones**
- *Miracle question:* If overnight a miracle happened and you reached your goal / solved your problem, what would be different?
- *1-10 scales:* How would you rate yourself on…? How important is…? How much energy do you have for…? How committed are you to…?
- *24 hours:* What could you do in the next 24 hours?
- *Without failure:* If you knew you couldn't fail, what would you do?

**NOW**
- What brings you here today? How would you describe your situation? What would you like to get from today?
- Describe your three biggest achievements: what makes them stand out? What did you learn?
- What energizes you? How are you contributing? What situations bring out the best in you?
- Who are the key people supporting you? Who else could help you?
- What have you started to put in motion? What would you prefer to have? What goals have you considered?
- What ideas do you have? Which is the best? What are your options?
- How do you see this situation? How does it sound to you? How does that feel?

**What If… (reflection)**
- I wonder what would happen if… · What if you had three wishes?
- If you do or don't take this action, what could happen? How good or bad would it be?
- If things went your way, what would be different?
- Could you treat this as an experiment and see what happens?

**Future**
- What would you like to achieve in the next [period]? What will you do differently? What do you hope to gain?
- If you took a step toward your dream, what would it be? What will be different after?
- What would a good outcome look like? Where do you want to be in a year?
- How perfect does the solution need to be? What is "good enough"?
- How will you feel about this decision looking back on it in two years?

**Action**
- When do you start? What small action can you take in the next [timeframe]? What is the next step before we meet again?
- What has to happen for you to get more of what you want?
- If you really want this, why haven't you started? *(use tactfully, curious tone)*
- What would be a quick solution and what would be more permanent? What would return control to you?

**About the Coaching Process**
- How would others know that coaching worked? What will be different if our sessions work?
- What sparked your desire to change? What are you really committed to?
- Should we focus on X or on Y?
- What are the most valuable ideas or techniques you've gained?
- Acting as your own coach, how will you maintain and strengthen what you've achieved?

## D. Vogt, Brown, and Isaacs — Architecture of Powerful Questions
**Three Dimensions**
1. **Construction:** most powerful are What? How? Why?; least powerful are yes/no, Which? Who? When? Where? The "whys" require genuine curiosity to not generate defense.
   Example progression: "Are you satisfied with…?" → "When have you been most satisfied?" → "What about this is most satisfying to you?" → "Why has it been up and down?"
2. **Scope:** adjust the question to a manageable level (local → organizational → systemic); avoid unmanageable scopes.
3. **Assumptions:** almost every question carries implicit beliefs. "What did we do wrong and who is responsible?" (assumes error/blame) vs. "What can we learn and what possibilities do we see?"

**7 Criteria to Evaluate a Question (agent's self-assessment before asking)**
1. Is it relevant to the person's real life?
2. Is it genuine (I really don't know the answer)?
3. What "work" do I want it to do (what conversation/feelings will it evoke)?
4. Is it familiar enough to understand and new enough to think differently?
5. What assumptions does it carry embedded?
6. Does it generate hope, imagination, and action, or increase focus on past problems?
7. Does it leave room for new questions?

**Game Plan (strategic life/project inquiry)**
1. Assess the current situation and formulate findings as questions (not complaints).
2. Discover 3 to 5 "big questions" that, if answered, would make the biggest difference.
3. Create images of possibility (scenarios if those questions were answered).
4. Evolve viable strategies and action plans, with continuous feedback cycle.

**"For All Occasions" Questions**
*Focus:*
- What question, if answered, would make the biggest difference for [your situation]?
- What is important to you about this and why does it matter to you?
- What attracts you to this inquiry? What is the deeper purpose (the big "why")?
- What opportunities do you see? What do you know so far and what is left to learn? What are the dilemmas?
- What assumptions do you need to test? What would someone with very different beliefs say?

*Connect ideas and deepen:*
- What is taking shape? What do you hear beneath what you're saying?
- What is emerging? What new connections are you making?
- What surprised or challenged you? What is missing from this picture? What aren't you seeing?
- What has been your biggest learning so far?
- What is the next level of thinking you need to do?
- If there were one thing still unsaid to reach greater clarity, what would it be?

*Drive action:*
- What would it take to create change in this?
- What could happen that would commit and energize you completely?
- What is possible here and who cares? (instead of "what is wrong and who is responsible?")
- What requires your immediate attention?
- If success were guaranteed, what bold steps would you choose?
- How can we support each other to take the next steps? What unique contribution do you make?
- What challenges could appear and how would you face them?
- What conversation, if started today, would create new possibilities? What seed could you plant today?

## E. AUDIT SUPPLEMENTS — Vogt, Brown, and Isaacs (14-page document, 10 sections; verified)

### E.1 The 10 Characteristics of a Powerful Question
A powerful question: generates curiosity in the listener · stimulates reflective conversation · provokes thought · brings underlying assumptions to the surface · invites creativity and new possibilities · generates energy and movement forward · channels attention and focuses inquiry · stays with participants · touches deep meaning · evokes more questions.
It also has the ability to **"travel well"**: extends beyond where it was born into broader networks of conversation.

### E.2 Recognizing a Powerful Question: The "Olympic Judge" Exercise
Rate from 1 to 10 the power of these questions: *What time is it?* · *Did you shower?* · *What possibilities exist that we haven't thought of?* · *What does it mean to be ethical?* According to the authors, across different cultures people almost always score the first two low (data and routine) and the last two high (possibility and meaning). **Idea:** powerful questions transcend borders. *Agent use:* before asking, imagine "rating it" from 1 to 10.

### E.3 Why We Don't Ask Better Questions (cultural diagnosis, paraphrased)
- Western culture and its education prioritize **memorizing and having "the right answer"**, so doubt and "not knowing" generate discomfort.
- There is attachment to **black/white thinking** and **quick solutions**; the pace of life leaves little room for reflective conversations before deciding.
- There is belief that the "real work" is analyzing and deciding fast, not inquiring deeply.
- Reward systems prize fixing problems more than innovative thinking.
- *Use in coaching:* explains why many people arrive wanting "the answer" and why space needs to be given to not knowing.

### E.4 Examples Illustrating the Power of Framing (summary)
- **Hewlett-Packard Labs:** the initial question "How to be the best laboratory *in* the world?" changed, by an engineer's suggestion, to "How to be the best *for* the world?". **A change of preposition** broadened the meaning and embedded assumptions and released a surge of collective energy; the question "traveled well" throughout the company.
- **Nobel and leader questions:** the table in the document shows how a well-formulated question (Watson and Crick on DNA structure; Johnson & Johnson's director in the Tylenol crisis; Ray Kroc) directed key results.
- **Danish school case:** "What else could be a good school?" as an open question that invites imagination.
- *Use in coaching:* help the person **reformulate their question** (changing a word can change meaning: "How to compete?" → "How to collaborate?").

### E.5 Four Capabilities of Those Who Lead with Questions (adapted from "leadership")
1. **Ask strategic questions** that open possibilities rather than just solve problems; be comfortable with not knowing and help others draw out their knowledge.
2. **Convene and sustain learning conversations** in a climate of safety and trust that reduces fear. Skills: create climate of discovery, suspend premature judgment, explore assumptions and beliefs, listen for connections between ideas, foster diverse perspectives, honor contributions, articulate shared understanding, harvest and share findings.
3. **Include diverse perspectives** (be a "connector" of people and ideas).
4. **Support appreciative inquiry** (Cooperrider): move from focusing only on what's not working to discovering and leveraging what is working as the basis for the future. Principle cited: *"Human systems grow toward what they persistently ask about"*. Model question: **"What is possible here and who cares?"** instead of "What is wrong here and who is to blame?".
- *Use in coaching:* alternate problem questions with questions about **what already works** (strengths, exceptions, previous successes).

### E.6 Diagnosis "Is Your Organization an Inquiry System?" (adaptable to teams and families)
