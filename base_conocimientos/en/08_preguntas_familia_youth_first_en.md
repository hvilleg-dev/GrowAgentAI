# 08 — Deep questions for adolescents and family (Youth First)

> Source: "50 thought-provoking questions to ask your teen" (Youth First, social workers; for parents and caregivers using them at home). Verified declared purpose of the organization: strengthen young people and families with evidence-based programs that prevent substance abuse, promote healthy behaviors, and maximize academic success. **The document only lists the questions: it includes no advice on listening, tone, or timing.** The 50 questions are in the secondary/high school section.
> **Note:** the original order is a continuous list. The grouping by categories below is own organization to facilitate agent use; the questions are from the document (translated).
> **Agent use:** with adolescents directly or to guide parents/caregivers on how to converse. Better in calm moments, without rush or interrogation or evaluation tone. Let the young person elaborate without interrupting.

## How to use them (guide for the agent and for parents) `[Own criterion: the source provides no instructions]`
- **One question per conversation**, not a battery. Choose according to the teen's mood.
- **Answer too** when it feels natural (model openness); don't turn the chat into a quiz.
- **Don't correct or preach** about their answer; thank them and ask "tell me more."
- **Questions with emotional risk** (fear, regret, happiness, worst experience): ask only with trust already built; if signs appear of depression, self-harm, abuse, or substance use, validate and guide toward a trusted adult or professional.
- **Questions with playful trick** (hypothetical): serve as door to serious topics (money, values, leadership).

## 1. Identity, strengths, and achievements
- Describe yourself in 5 words or less.
- What is your greatest strength and your greatest weakness?
- What is the achievement you're most proud of and why?
- What are 3 really small victories you've had in recent days that you're proud of?
- Do you think it's better to have one great skill where you excel (A+) or many skills where you're average (C), and why?
- If you could become famous as an adult, why would you be famous?
- If you could have only one superpower, what would it be?
- If you had no physical, financial, or other obstacles, what skill would you like to have?
- Which of your friends do you think I'd like most and why?

## 2. Values, purpose, and sense of life
- What do you feel are the most important things to live a great life?
- What have you learned in life that you feel will be most useful to you?
- If we lost everything we own today but could keep 3 things, what would you choose and why?
- Do you believe money can or cannot buy happiness?
- How would you explain the word "love" to someone without using the word "love"?
- If they told you that you would live forever, what would you change about your life?
- If you had enough money to never have to work, what would you do with your time?
- What has had the greatest impact on your life, good or bad, and why?
- When do you feel that a child or adolescent becomes an adult?

## 3. Service, gratitude, and impact on others
- What are 3 things you're grateful for in your life and why?
- What is something you did in the last 24 hours to make someone's life better?
- How can you help someone today?
- How would you change the world?
- If you could change any 3 things in the world, what would they be and why?
- If you could give every person in the world a gift, but it had to be the same gift, what would it be?
- If you could make one rule that everyone in the world had to follow, what would it be?
- When someone feels stressed, what are 3 suggestions you'd share with that person to feel less stressed?

## 4. Fears, failures, habits, and obstacles *(requires trust)*
- What are you most afraid of?
- What do you think prevents you from achieving complete happiness?
- Think about the worst thing that's happened to you… what did you learn from it?
- If we experience our greatest moments of growth and learning from failure, why do we fear failure so much?
- Have you let slip an opportunity you now regret? What were they?
- Do you have any bad habits you wish you could break? What are they?
- What do you do when you can't sleep at night?
- If you could travel back in time 3 years, what advice would you give yourself?
- What hobby don't you have now that you'd love to learn? What prevents you from doing it?

## 5. Family and relationships
- What makes our family amazing, something you feel is unique in us?
- If you could set a rule for the family that we all had to follow, what would it be?
- If you could have dinner with 3 people you don't know, who would they be and where would you go?
- *(With coaching intention)* After any of these answers: "What would you like us to do more of as a family?"

## 6. School, work, and future
- Of all the things you're learning, which do you think will be most useful to you as an adult?
- Imagine that tomorrow you're the teacher at school. What are 3 things you'd teach to make school more useful for someone's future?
- Would you prefer being the boss or the employee, and what would make you better in one position versus the other?
- Imagine you're the President and need 3 people to assist you. Who would you choose and why?
- Would you rather have: 1 million dollars today or 1 penny doubled every day for 30 days? *(teaches compound interest: opens conversation about money and patience)*

## 7. Lifestyle and imagination
- Describe what a perfect day would be like from when you wake up until bedtime.
- If you could live anywhere in the world, where would it be? · Where in the world would you most like to live?
- What is the best invention you've seen recently and what do you like about it?
- What do you feel is the hardest job in the world and why?
- What product would you stock up on if you heard it was no longer going to be sold?

## 8. Coaching bridges for parents and youth leaders
After the teen responds, the adult can use (without judgment):
- "Tell me more about that."
- "What matters most to you about what you just said?"
- "What would you like to be different?"
- "What small step could you take this week?"
- "How can I support you in that?"
